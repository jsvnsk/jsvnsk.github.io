# Period Music from *Jane's World War II Fighters*

*Jane's World War II Fighters* is a combat flight simulator game released in 1998 for the Windows 95/98 platform. The game features an award-winning original musical score comprised of contemporary Classical music composed by Don Veca.

It also contains Big Band and Swing Jazz music embedded in a game binary file named `museum.elb` which appears on game disk #2. The individual music tracks can be extracted from the game binary file and saved to `.WAV` files using a utility named `ELBzip`. The `ELBzip` utility can be downloaded from the [ww2fighters.org](https://www.ww2fighters.org/) website. The `.WAV` files created by `ELBzip` may contain some audible glitches.

# The Mysterious "Unknown" Music Tracks

The game features 5 music tracks which are not associated with actual period music. These are named `BIGBANDBOOGIE.WAV`, `FLYINGHIGH.WAV`, `JANESSTOMP.WAV`, `OVERBOARD.WAV`, and `ZATARAIN.WAV`. Several internet sources incorrectly attribute `ZATARAIN.WAV` with *"Saturday Night Fish Fry"* by Louis Jordan and his Tympany Five.

The mystery behind `ZATARAIN.WAV` helps unlock the secrets of the other 4 "unknown" music tracks. The short answer: it is definitely **not** *"Saturday Night Fish Fry"*. It's not even a song by Louis Jordan. In fact, `ZATARAIN.WAV` isn't any other mainstream song released by a commercial artist. Rather, like the other 4 uncredited tracks, it is production music composed explicitly to mimic the style of the early 1940s. As such, these 5 files are "stock" music belonging to either APM Music, Extreme Music, or EA's internal audio department.

The following breakdown examines how the musical mix-up involving `ZATARAIN.WAV` probably happened and why it is likely named after a popular New Orleans brand of rice seasoning.

**1. Stylistic Similarities**

The `ZATARAIN.WAV` audio track utilizes the same fast-paced jump blues tempo, boogie-woogie piano riffs, and swinging horn sections as Jordan's *"Saturday Night Fish Fry"*. In fact, it acts as a meticulous "pastiche", reproducing the precise acoustic texture, instrumentation, and energetic performance style of the original, right down to the swing rhythm and horn arrangements. Because production music libraries hire professional studio musicians specifically to capture the distinct sonic signature of legendary artists, casual listeners can easily confuse this soundalike with Louis Jordan's original track.

**2. Internet Misinformation**

Unfortunately, in the information age, well-published "guesses" often become accepted as "facts", especially if they are copied among various websites. Because the track is so similar to *"Saturday Night Fish Fry"*, players of *Jane's WWII Fighters* may have assumed it was the original Louis Jordan song. It is likely someone posted the incorrect information to IMDb or a gaming wiki decades ago, establishing an incorrect record that subsequent websites have simply duplicated.

**3. The Name is a Cooking Pun**

*Zatarain's* is a famous American food brand, renowned for its Jambalaya Rice Mix and also its seafood breading mix called *"Wonderful Fish-Fri"*. The developers playfully named the file `ZATARAIN` as an internal joke, substituting words from the name of the song's inspiration ("Fish Fry") with the famous brand that makes "Fish-Fri".

**4. Why Acoustic Fingerprinting Tools Like Shazam Fail to Identify ZATARAIN.WAV and the Other "Mystery" Tracks**

Acoustic fingerprinting tools rely on databases of commercially released singles, albums, and streaming platforms. Because these works were never sold as commercial songs on iTunes, Spotify, or vinyl, Shazam has no fingerprint for them to match against.

# Summary

These 5 files are *"stock"* music tracks custom-made to give *Jane's WWII Fighters* an authentic 1940s big-band/jump-blues atmosphere. Historically, Jordan's *"Saturday Night Fish Fry"* was not released until 1949, well after the conclusion of WWII. New Orleans food history matches this timeline anomaly, as Zatarain's did not acquire the *"Wonderful Fish-Fri"* product until 1963.
